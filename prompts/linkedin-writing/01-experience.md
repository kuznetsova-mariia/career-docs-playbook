# LinkedIn Experience — Describing What You Did

This file is about the **content** of your work history: how to fill in each
role so it reads as real, owned achievements. Making the profile **findable**
(headline, About, skills, keywords) is a separate step — see
`02-profile-seo.md`.

## Positioning first
Open with where you're aiming, and let every role serve that story. Example:
"I want to grow toward a senior/lead-level software engineer, focused more on
technical development." Without it, the profile reads as "about everything
and nothing."

## One role at a time
Describe each job separately, not as one wall of text. A reader should be
able to scan a single position and understand what you did there.

## Use STAR (condensed)
Frame each item with the STAR method:
- **Situation** — the context or problem you faced.
- **Task** — what you were responsible for in it.
- **Action** — what you actually did (the bulk of the value; lead here).
- **Result** — the outcome, ideally with a number.

Keep it tight — a line or two per achievement, not a paragraph of context.
On LinkedIn, weight the Action and Result; trim the Situation to a few words.

## Same craft as the resume
Power verbs and the XYZ formula apply exactly as on the CV:
- Power verbs — `../cv-writing/02-power-verbs.md`
- XYZ formula — `../cv-writing/03-xyz-formula.md`
- Metric categories — `../cv-writing/01-achievements-numbers.md`

Every bullet should carry an outcome, a metric, and the tools involved:
what you did, what changed, which technologies.

## Lead with action, not responsibility
Replace passive, presence-only phrasing — "responsible for", "involved in",
"supported", "worked on" — with action verbs that show you drove the result:
**led**, **built**, **executed**, **delivered**, **scaled**. What matters is
what you *did*, not what you were *assigned to*.
