# US Job Search Tactics

## It's a numbers game
- Expect hundreds of applications (400–1000+ is normal); target ~50/day in
  active mode. 6+ months of searching is a realistic horizon.
- Apply early: vacancies are usually posted at the start of the workday, and
  position in the response queue matters. Answer recruiter emails instantly
  with a prepared template + resume attached.
- Q1 is the dead season; hiring picks up later in the year.

## Search beyond LinkedIn
Google X-ray over ATS platforms — vacancies appear there before job boards:

```
site:lever.co | site:greenhouse.io | site:jobs.ashbyhq.com | site:app.dover.io
("Project Manager" | "Program Manager") (remote | location)
-"customer success" after:2026-05-01
```

- `after:` filters stale postings; `-"..."` removes irrelevant roles.
- Greenhouse also has an internal job base with filters — and increasingly
  CAPTCHAs and "no AI tools" pledges, so pure auto-apply is fading.
- Job boards: Indeed, Glassdoor, Dice (critical for QA/engineering contract
  market — keep the profile keyword-rich and refresh it daily so it ranks),
  Monster, CareerBuilder, Built In, Wellfound (startups), YC Jobs.
- VC portfolio pages double as job boards: Sequoia, a16z, Accel, Kleiner
  Perkins, Bessemer, Lightspeed, Greylock, Index, Founders Fund + Y
  Combinator and Techstars portfolios.

## Where to live matters
Most US tech vacancies concentrate in: SF Bay Area, Seattle, NYC, Boston,
LA, Washington D.C., Austin (growing: Miami, Denver, Phoenix, Houston).
More companies = more events = better network = more shots on goal. Remote
listings are shrinking and more competitive; hybrid and
"within drivable distance" are the trend. Recruiters prioritize locals —
set your location accordingly.

## Referrals at scale
Ask for referrals wherever possible — including community-run referral
spreadsheets where strangers refer each other into big tech.

## Don't argue with the system
The hiring market is a mating dance: the rules are partly arbitrary, but
they are learnable. Being right wins arguments, not offers. Learn the rules
and play.
