# Interview Prep

Preparing is not optional. Some walk in cold; others come knowing the
company, the industry, and the role — and get the offers.

## Research the company with an LLM

> I'm preparing for an interview for the [TITLE] position at [COMPANY NAME].
> Please conduct an in-depth analysis of the company and the role:
> 1. General information: employees, locations, revenue, products, mission.
> 2. Market position: competitors, unique value propositions, recent news.
> 3. Corporate culture: employee reviews (Glassdoor, Reddit), work environment.
> 4. Role information: responsibilities, required skills, growth opportunities.
> 5. Industry trends: current trends, challenges, opportunities.
> 6. Interview preparation: common questions for this role, tips for strong answers, smart questions to ask the interviewer.
> 7. Additional: interesting facts, recent projects.
>
> Provide the information in a well-structured format.

If the company name is generic, add the city, state, or ZIP. Cross-check
manually: company site (About / Products), Crunchbase (funding), LinkedIn
(team growth, hiring), Glassdoor / Reddit (real culture), G2 (what clients
think), YouTube (interviews with leadership).

## Answer frameworks
- **STAR** (Situation → Task → Action → Result) — universal.
- **PAR** (Problem → Action → Result) — one short paragraph; great for
  emails and quick answers.
- US communication style: confidence and positivity, short clear answers
  without excess detail, focus on measurable impact.
- Do mock interviews in English with people who work in the target market.
  Confidence and calm at the interview matter as much as correctness.

## "Why did you leave?"
A standard question; short tenures (often <3 years) get extra scrutiny.
Prepare an answer per role — three honest framings:
1. **Growth:** "I was offered a position that aligned more closely with my
   long-term goals and gave a clear path for growth in [area]."
2. **Project-based:** "Once the key milestones were delivered, the project
   entered maintenance; I thrive in dynamic environments and went looking
   for the next challenge."
3. **Cultural fit:** "Over time I realized the company's direction didn't
   fully align with my professional goals; I left on good terms for a
   stronger mutual fit."

Show the short stints were conscious decisions with lessons learned.
Rehearse the answer; have it written down.

## Thank-you email
Send after the interview. The key: two or three PAR stories matching what
the company is looking for.

> Subject: Thank You for the Opportunity
>
> Hello [Name], thank you for the engaging discussion during my interview
> for the [role] at [Company]. I would like to highlight two experiences
> that align closely with the responsibilities of the role:
> At [Company 1], [PAR story 1]. At [Company 2], [PAR story 2].
> I am excited about the possibility of bringing my experience to [Company].
> I look forward to potentially working together!

## After a rejection
Send a post-rejection letter: thank them for the process, restate interest,
offer to stay in touch for other roles. If the chosen candidate falls
through, you're the first one they call back. A rejection is sometimes just
a delayed yes.
