# On the Job: Growing After You're Hired

## 8 markers you're in the right place
Deep satisfaction from impact and growth; a boss who invests in you and
pulls you up; income that funds a good life and double-digit savings; your
talents used 80%+ of the time (amplifying strengths, not patching
weaknesses); clear 3–5 year goals the current role serves; a growing
industry, company, and team with business-critical scope; a culture of
experiments and meritocracy with inspiring colleagues; mostly complex,
creative, leadership-demanding tasks. If two or more are missing — don't
endure it indefinitely.

## 1:1s with a US manager
A 1:1 is relationship management, not a status update.
- Prepare small talk (US offices run on it). Pick a recurring personal
  "character" — the yoga person, the sci-fi person — it makes you easy to
  remember and talk to.
- Don't list 17 small tasks. Tell one solid achievement with detail: what
  you did, why it was hard, what the impact was. Then: "I think this is a
  good case to show the department and your skip."
- Ask regularly: what should I improve, where do I need more visibility,
  which skills matter for the next level.

## Taking criticism with a cold head
A surprise meeting appears with your manager and someone else — someone will
complain. Don't rush to write defensive messages before the call. On the
call: listen, don't counterattack, take time — "Thank you for sharing this.
I'd like to review it carefully and follow up with questions." After: if the
facts are on your side, a dry factual email to your manager, CC the
initiator and their manager, ending with a light question ("Have we diverged
from the strategy we discussed in the fall?"). On your next 1:1 — no
complaining; propose three process improvements. You're not defending
yourself; you're raising effectiveness.

## Are you a marginal hire?
AI shifts hiring: roles that teams can live without simply stop being
opened. Self-check: If I left, would something stop working? Is my job
decisions or task execution? Do I build the system or just operate it? Does
my work touch the company's money? If they could automate me tomorrow, would
they? Anxious answers = move toward ownership and business impact.

## When you leave: the hand-off doc
Always write one — for them and for you. Structure: short context of your
role; recurring stakeholders; active projects with links (PRDs, epics,
dashboards, status, risks); your personal contacts. Keep it short — an index
of artifacts, not a book. It cements your reputation after you're gone and
doubles as a ready-made achievements inventory for your resume.

## Skills that compound
The breakthrough meta-skills: managing your state (rest deliberately, work
like a human, not a machine), self-motivation, and deep work (knowing your
peak hours, entering focus, not breaking it). Useful soft-skill frameworks
to look up: HALT (hungry/angry/lonely/tired — don't decide in those states),
FAIR (facts → affect → interests → request) for conflicts, SCARF for status
dynamics.
