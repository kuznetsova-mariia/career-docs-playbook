# Networking and Referrals

Hiring works on "trust the known": warm contacts, referrals, and shared
history beat cold applications. Your goal is to be known before you apply —
not to be one application out of a thousand.

## What gets people hired
Looking at who actually gets in: experience at a direct competitor, a former
colleague who brought them in, or a referral community. No US network yet?
- Worked at a global company — find colleagues from other countries who
  moved; the shared brand is your opening line.
- US school alumni respond even to strangers.
- Go offline: meetups, career events. Prepare a 2–3 sentence elevator pitch.

## How to write to people (Carnegie, adapted)
Don't open with "please look at my resume". Any good US communication
starts with interest in the recipient:
1. **Show interest:** "Adam, I saw you worked on X at Y. I'm doing something
   similar in Z — how did you approach that solution?"
2. **Friendly tone** beats formal requests.
3. **Name + details** — show you did your homework; no copy-paste blasts.
4. **Let them talk about themselves** — ask about their challenges first.
5. **Talk about what interests them:** shared events, conferences, courses.
6. **Make them feel valued:** like a recent post, endorse skills, leave a
   substantive comment before messaging.

## Find hiring managers
- Company page → People tab. For a Software Engineer look for: VP of
  Engineering, Engineering Manager, Head/Director of Engineering, Technical
  Recruiter. Follow at least five, save the profiles.
- LinkedIn search: `hiring software engineer` (your role), filter Posts →
  Top match — real people hiring right now.
- Google Boolean for target companies:
  `site:linkedin.com/in/ ("Engineering Manager" OR "Director of Engineering"
  OR "VP Engineering") "at Company"`.
- Then warm up: comment on their posts meaningfully ("This aligns closely
  with what I've been working on — thanks for sharing"), so your name is
  familiar before you reach out.

## Mind the limits
LinkedIn caps invites (~50/day; bursts look like a bot). Add people in small
sessions. Mass complaints, shared accounts, and VPN games risk a ban.
