## Ready-made prompt: writing viral posts
Paste this to your LLM, then add your raw material. Inspired by Ivan
Doronin's viral-posts prompt.

> You are my LinkedIn ghostwriter. Write a viral LinkedIn post from the raw
> material I give you, following these rules strictly:
>
> 1. **Hook in the first 8–12 words.** Use one of three types: a personal
>    action with a number, a recognizable pain with an unexpected cause, or a
>    counterintuitive fact.
> 2. **Break the expected pattern in the first third** — a turn like "but the
>    interesting part is elsewhere", or an admitted mistake.
> 3. **Concrete numbers and named specifics**, never abstractions ("180
>    applications", not "many").
> 4. **One honest, vulnerable moment** — a doubt, a failure, a real lesson.
>    Not a flawless success story.
> 5. **Meat**: a concrete situation that actually happened. If it's under
>    NDA, anonymize the company and extract the principle.
> 6. **End with one open question** as the CTA. No "like and subscribe", no
>    hashtags, no links in the body.
> 7. **200–500 words**, short sentences, generous white space, clean
>    grammar, minimal emojis.
> 8. **Sound like me, not like an LLM.** Match my voice from these samples:
>    [paste 1–2 of your past posts, or describe your tone].
>
> My positioning / niche: [e.g. senior/lead software engineer, technical focus].
> Raw material: [describe what happened, with real details and numbers].
>
> Ask me clarifying questions if the material is thin — don't invent facts.

## How the algorithm weighs engagement
Save ×10, share to DMs ×8, comment ×5 (longer weighs more), "see more"
click ×2 (dwell time), like ×1. Write what people want to save and forward:
checklists, breakdowns, personal stories with a takeaway.

## Three phases of a post
1. **0–60 min** — test show to a small slice of your network. 3+ comments in
   the first hour multiply reach. Reply to comments — replies restart the
   algorithm.
2. **1–8 h** — friends of friends; the main growth mechanism.
3. **8–72 h** — long tail; a good post lives 3–5 days and can hit Suggested.

## Format
- Carousel (PDF) — the strongest format; native video under 30 seconds with
  subtitles; text + a photo of people.
- External links cut reach hard — put them in comments. Hashtags barely work.
- Don't edit after publishing (resets the algorithm). One post a day max;
  three thoughtful posts a week beat seven shallow ones.

## What makes a post work
- **Hook in the first 8–12 words.** Three types that work: a personal action
  with a number; a recognizable pain with an unexpected cause; a
  counterintuitive fact.
- **Pattern break in the first third** — "but the interesting part is
  elsewhere", or admitting a mistake.
- **Concrete numbers**, not abstractions: not "many responses" — 180.
- **Sincerity** — honest doubt or failure posts sustain engagement for
  months. Don't make every case a flawless success story.
- **Series beat single posts** — regularity in one niche builds topic
  authority.
- **One CTA** — an open question, not "like and subscribe".
- **Meat** — concrete situations that happened to you, not abstract advice.
  Under NDA: anonymize the company, extract the principles.
- Every post must contain *you*: your take, your role, your lesson.
- Optimal length 200–500 words, short sentences, lots of white space,
  clean grammar, minimal emojis. Personal profiles far outreach company
  pages; you don't need a huge following.

## Self-presentation templates
Have a ready answer to "what do you do":
- **One-liner:** "I'm a marketer who makes brands visible on Instagram."
- **Value + action:** "I help founders package products and build their
  social media communication."
- **Expert intro:** "10 years in IT. Led projects that grew to 100K users."
- **Mini-story:** "Came into digital marketing by accident; went from intern
  to team lead in three years."
- **Personal slogan:** "I help ideas find their audience."

Structure: who you are, what you do, what value you bring — plus one
personal note.
