# US Resume Rules

## Rewrite, don't translate
A resume for the US market is written from scratch, not translated. Read 10
job descriptions for your target role and borrow their language: which words
repeat, which skills they ask for, what results they consider significant.

## Job title
- Use standardized, searchable titles: Developer → Software Engineer,
  Events Manager → Field Marketing Manager.
- Validate on LinkedIn Jobs: search your title with the United States filter.
  1,500+ openings — good title; ~200 — too rare, pick another.
- Add a specialization after a comma — it's a free keyword slot:
  `Software Engineer, API`; `Marketing Manager, Paid Ads`.
- On your last position, the title must match how vacancies name the role
  (US background checks verify employment, not exact titles).

## Format
- Write in Google Docs, send as PDF unless asked otherwise.
- No photo, date of birth, or gender. No columns, tables, or graphics.
  One font; bullets are the only formatting.
- US city and US phone number in the header (buy a forwarding number if
  you're not there yet).
- Keep one source of truth for versions — name files clearly.

## Length: Hook, Proof, Depth
One page is not a law. Every page must earn its place:
1. **Hook** — the summary at the top: who you are and why you're worth
   reading further. A 3-line pitch.
2. **Proof** — achievements, results, measurable impact.
3. **Depth** — education, skills, projects that back everything above.

Cut ancient irrelevant experience; keep anything that still echoes.

## Look expensive
The market pays for packaging, not raw skills. Want +20–30%? Open vacancies
one level above your current range — the ones that don't invite you — and
study their vocabulary. Rewrite your experience in that language: business
context, influence, outcomes — not task lists. Your resume should read one
level above where you are.

## Ready-made LLM prompts
Replace the brackets with your data; always edit the output.

> **Spot the flaws:** Act as a recruiter for [specific role/title]. Review my
> resume below and highlight weak areas, overused buzzwords, and missing
> metrics. Be brutally honest. Provide 3 different options to choose from.

> **Rewrite for impact:** Rewrite this resume to sound more results-driven,
> quantifiable, and compelling for [specific role/title]. Focus on
> achievements, not just duties.

> **ATS boost:** Update this resume to be fully optimized for Applicant
> Tracking Systems (ATS) for the role of [specific role/title]. Use
> industry-specific keywords naturally.

> **Craft my hook:** Write a powerful, 3-line professional summary that hooks
> a recruiter in under 10 seconds. Prioritize impact, clarity, and value.

> **Transferable skills:** Please analyze my resume and this job description.
> What transferable or adjacent skills do I have that match this role?
> Highlight the top 3 relevant skills from my experience that align with this
> job, and explain how they match.

You don't need a 100% match with a vacancy. What matters is translating your
experience into the language of the target role.
